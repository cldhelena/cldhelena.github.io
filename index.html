Data Hubs Built with Lovable

3–4 dashboards → 1 hub
Situation

Teams had to open three or four separate Power BI dashboards to see their own KPIs. Power BI was great for analysis, but it couldn't write back to the CRM, send alerts, or adapt to how each team worked.

Problem

No single view per team, and no way to act on the data from the screen where it was shown. The people who needed it were non-technical managers, not analysts.

What I built

Replaced the dashboards with internal data hubs built with Lovable on top of a BigQuery data layer: one place per team for the onboarding funnel, late-onboarding follow-up, channels, and team productivity, plus a Data FAQ explaining the logic behind every KPI. Data modeling stayed in the warehouse, and AI handled only the application layer. Access is secured with company-email login, and some hubs include an AI assistant so managers can ask questions about their own data.

AI · Agentic Automation

Churn Intelligence System

5 days → 0 manual work
Situation

There was no systematic process for churn classification. Every month, someone spent 5 days manually reading diagnostic data, interpreting it subjectively, and consolidating the results. The process was inconsistent between cycles and never improved.

Problem

Classifying churn drivers required human reading of raw diagnostic data, subjective interpretation, and error-prone manual consolidation. Reports arrived too late to be actionable, and accuracy didn't compound over time.

What I built

An agentic pipeline in N8N with two AI agents: a Classifier that reads diagnostic data and maps churn drivers automatically, and an AI Judge (Claude) that reviews outputs, detects inconsistencies, and auto-corrects before delivery to Slack on the last day of every month. A separate audit module via Claude Code calculates classification accuracy each cycle, surfaces errors, and generates a training spreadsheet — the system gets measurably smarter with every run, without human intervention.

N8N Claude Slack API Google Sheets Claude Code
GTM Engineering · AI Automation

Contact Enrichment Flow

2x leads with verified contacts
Situation

The GTM team operated in a highly specific niche — traditional enrichment tools like Apollo failed to find quality contacts. The prospect list existed (companies), but finding the right person inside each company was a manual, low-yield process.

Problem

Standard enrichers couldn't navigate the niche. Finding contacts required manual research per company, with inconsistent results and no scalability. The ICP was well-defined but impossible to execute at volume.

What I built

An AI agent running Claude inside Clay that iterates over a company leads spreadsheet and autonomously scrapes reliable web sources to find contacts matching a pre-defined ICP (role, seniority, department). Each contact exits with phone, email, and LinkedIn profile. Clay feeds that data into an N8N workflow that generates a personalized briefing for each contact and triggers a custom outbound email cadence — personalised by what was found about the person, not just their title.

Revenue Intelligence · BI Engineering

Events ROI Dashboard

Vanity metrics → ROI
Situation

The company ran events across multiple markets (Latin America, North America, Europe) with no financial return visibility. Performance was measured in attendance and reach — metrics that had no connection to pipeline or revenue.

Problem

There was no way to know which events generated real pipeline, which had positive payback, and which were consuming budget without return. Budget allocation was based on intuition and attendance numbers, not financial sustainability.

What I built

An end-to-end funnel model from event attendance through to closed revenue, with payback modeling (time-to-recover-investment) calculated per event type and per market. A single source of truth that lets Product and Business teams answer one question: is this event actually worth running again? Shifted the internal conversation from reach to return.

Principles

How I Think About Data

01

Data without business context doesn't generate decisions — it generates confusion. Every model I build starts with: what will someone actually do with this?

02

Real automation includes the feedback loop — a pipeline that runs but never improves is just deferred manual work. I build systems that audit themselves.

03

Historical accuracy is not optional — if your data model doesn't handle organizational changes correctly, your metrics are lying to you.

04

Frameworks fail without context — RevOps playbooks, data mesh principles, agile sprints: all useful, all wrong when applied without understanding the specific business reality.
